Most spood health problems come down to a handful of causes: dehydration, failed molts, falls, and prey injuries. The good news — early signs are visible, and most issues are preventable with routine care.

## The daily health scan (30 seconds)

During feeding and misting, glance for:

- **Abdomen shape:** gently rounded is healthy. Wrinkled or shrunken suggests dehydration; overly plump suggests overfeeding.
- **Posture and movement:** a healthy spood stands with legs under it, tracks prey, and grooms.
- **Appetite:** refusal for a day or two often just means premolt — check the abdomen before worrying.

## Common issues

### Dehydration

**Signs:** wrinkled abdomen, sluggishness, curled legs late-stage.
**Response:** mist immediately and offer droplets near the spood; most bounce back within a day when caught early.

### Molt problems

**Signs:** stuck in old skin, half-emerged for hours.
**Response:** raise humidity, do not pull the old skin — pulling tears fragile new limbs. Give quiet time; some recover on their own.

### Falls

Jumpers handle small falls, but falls onto hard surfaces cause injuries.
**Prevention:** handle low over soft surfaces, keep enclosures away from edges, and use lids.

### Prey injuries

Prey that fights back (large crickets, hornets, stinging insects) can injure a spood.
**Prevention:** feed prey smaller than the abdomen, never leave aggressive prey overnight, stick to flies and soft-bodied prey.

## When it's beyond home care

An exotics vet with invertebrate experience is ideal but rare. Before that, experienced keeper communities (like the ones our community page links to) can help interpret what you're seeing. Describe the abdomen, posture, appetite, and last molt date — precise details get better answers.

## Preventive care wins

The boring routines are the real medicine: daily droplets, appropriately sized prey, undisturbed molts, clean enclosures, and gentle handling. Log everything in Polly's Web — a pattern ("not eating since last Tuesday") is exactly what turns a mystery into a diagnosis.
