Feeding is the heartbeat of spood care. A well-fed jumper is active, curious, and grows on schedule — and feeding time is the easiest way to bond with your spood.

## What to feed

Jumping spiders eat live prey they can overpower. The right staple depends on your spood's size:

- **Sling to early juvenile:** flightless fruit flies (hydei or melanogaster). Offer 2–4 flies every 2–3 days.
- **Juvenile to adult:** houseflies, blue/green bottle flies (mini size for smaller spoods), and small roaches like red runner nymphs. Offer prey roughly the size of your spood's abdomen or slightly smaller.
- **Occasional variety:** small waxworms (as a treat — high fat), tiny moths, and other soft-bodied prey.

Avoid prey that fights back hard (adult crickets can injure a resting spood) and never offer wild-caught prey unless you can guarantee it is pesticide-free.

## How often

- **Slings:** every 1–2 days, small portions.
- **Juveniles:** every 2–3 days.
- **Adults:** every 3–5 days. Adults can safely go a bit longer; a slightly round abdomen is healthy, a very plump one means it's time to ease off.

In Polly's Web, log each feeding and your reminder schedule adapts to your spood's life stage.

## Reading your spood at mealtime

- **Interested and alert:** they'll track the prey, crouch, and stalk. That's your green light.
- **Not interested:** a spood that ignores prey for a day is usually fine — check for premolt signs before offering again.
- **Premolt:** refusal to eat plus a dark, shiny abdomen means feeding should pause. Offering food during premolt can endanger a molting spood.

## Success matters more than offerings

A fly left in the enclosure overnight is not a meal — it's a roommate. Where you can, watch the catch and log whether the offer was *taken* (successful) or ignored. Tracking successful feedings gives you a far truer picture of your spood's health than tracking offers.

## Quick tips

- Feed with the lights on and during your spood's active hours.
- Shake a couple of flies into a small vial, then release near (not on) your spood.
- Remove live prey that hasn't been caught within a few hours.
- Log every offering — successful or not — so patterns surface early.
