Jumping spiders rarely drink from bowls — they sip droplets from surfaces. Hydration is less about equipment and more about a good misting routine.

## Misting: the daily ritual

Mist one side of the enclosure once a day (or every other day in humid climates) so your spood can choose between moist and dry areas. Spray a fine mist of dechlorinated water onto the enclosure wall and any plants — never directly at your spood.

- **Best time:** morning. Droplets persist through the day.
- **Best droplet size:** visible beads that cling to the glass — not a soaking film.
- **Water choice:** bottled spring water, boiled-and-cooled tap water, or dechlorinated tap water. Avoid distilled water alone for long periods (no minerals) and never use water with additives.

## Signs your spood is hydrated

- A gently rounded abdomen (not deflated or wrinkled).
- Regular activity and hunting interest.
- Drinking from droplets shortly after you mist — normal and good to watch for.

A shrunken or wrinkled abdomen can mean dehydration, premolt, or illness. Increase misting frequency and observe closely; if it persists, consult an experienced keeper or exotics vet.

## What to avoid

- **Water dishes:** deep dishes are drowning hazards. If you offer water in a container, use a shallow cap with pebbles or a cotton wick.
- **Wet cotton balls:** fibers can tangle tiny feet.
- **Soaking the substrate:** constant dampness invites mold and mites. The goal is droplets, not humidity trapped in bedding.

## Misting and molting

During premolt and molt, keep misting — many spoods drink more at this time — but avoid spraying a spood that has begun molting. Log each misting in Polly's Web so the pattern stays visible alongside feedings and molts.
