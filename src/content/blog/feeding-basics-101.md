Feeding is where new keepers overthink and experienced keepers under-fuss. Here's the working knowledge that covers 95% of situations.

## Buy the right prey

The shortlist, by spood size:

- **Slings and small juveniles:** flightless fruit flies (melanogaster for tiny slings, hydei for bigger ones).
- **Juveniles:** more hydei flies, green bottle flies (mini), red runner roach nymphs.
- **Adults:** houseflies, blue/green bottle flies, small roaches.

The rule of thumb: prey around the size of your spood's abdomen or smaller. Crickets are the classic beginner mistake — they fight back, bite, and can injure a spood between molts.

## Offer, watch, log

There's a difference between an *offering* and a *meal*. Shake a fly in, watch the pounce, and log what happened. Three data points beat one:

- **Taken:** appetite is good, schedule is right.
- **Ignored once:** normal — reoffer tomorrow.
- **Ignored repeatedly:** check for premolt (dark, glossy abdomen) before assuming anything is wrong.

In Polly's Web, feeding logs track offered vs. successful separately — the success rate is the signal worth watching.

## How often — the honest schedule

- **Slings:** every 1–2 days.
- **Juveniles:** every 2–3 days.
- **Adults:** every 3–5 days.

But the schedule serves the abdomen, not the other way around. Gently rounded = healthy. Very plump = cut back. Wrinkled = increase misting and check in.

## Feeding-time mistakes to avoid

- **Leaving prey in overnight.** A loose fly is stress, not dinner.
- **Feeding during premolt.** If they're refusing food and look dark/glossy, wait. Prey near a molting spood can be genuinely dangerous.
- **Handling right after the catch.** Let them eat in peace.
- **Free-handing flies into the enclosure.** Shake them into a vial first — you'll control the count and the release.

## The habit that ties it together

Feed at consistent times, in the same way, and log it. After a few weeks you'll have something no guide can give you: *your* spood's personal baseline — and every deviation from it becomes early information instead of a mystery.
