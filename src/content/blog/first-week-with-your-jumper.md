The first week with a new jumping spider sets the tone. Here's the calm, unhurried version of what to do — and what to skip.

## Day 1: Settle in, hands off

Your spood has traveled, been handled at a shop or shipped, and is now in a brand-new space. Set up the enclosure with a good anchor corner and a water misting, then… leave them alone. No handling, no rearranging. Their first job is building a hammock — their safe spot. Let them.

## Days 2–4: First meals

Once your spood is out of the hammock and tracking movement, offer the first food. Right size matters more than right schedule:

- Fruit flies for slings and small juveniles.
- Houseflies or mini bottle flies for bigger juveniles and adults.

Log every offering — taken or ignored. A spood that ignores prey for a day is usually fine; two days of refusal is worth checking for premolt signs.

## Days 4–7: Find the rhythm

By now you'll notice your spood's patterns: when they're active, where they perch, how they hunt. That's your care rhythm — feed during their active hours, mist in the morning so droplets last, and keep handling short and optional.

## Three things new keepers worry about (that are usually fine)

1. **"My spood isn't moving much today."** Spoods rest a lot, especially before and after meals. Check abdomen and posture; if they look normal, patience is the right move.
2. **"My spood stopped eating."** First question: is the abdomen dark and shiny? That's premolt, not illness — pause feeding and keep misting.
3. **"My spood built a weird little tent."** That's a hammock — exactly where they should be.

## The one habit worth starting now

Log care as it happens. A week of honest feeding, misting, and observation notes tells you more about a new spood's health than any single moment — and Polly's Web turns those notes into the patterns that keep them thriving.
